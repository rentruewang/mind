## Why rhetorical questions (irony) are annoying

They imply the listener "needs to be reminded", belittling the listener.

Now, it doesn't sound rude if you also bring yourself down ("who doesn't like money" -> shows signs of own weakness), but overall it can be annoying when you are reminding the other people the obvious ("don't you know...?", this is more annoying when you already know).

## Verbal irony and sarcasm

- Deduction
- Theory of mind (the speaker is _intentionally_ wrong).
- Context
- Generated by simulation (which is where the indirect / deduction part happens)

### Irony (contradiction)

Irony: indirect + contradiction

Per incongruity theory:

> Rediculous: inconsistent (incongruity theory) + stupid.

e.g. "I'm poor." "Nice shoes."
-> not poor
-> lying

e.g. "You're early"
-> Situation: late
-> No

> Backhanded compliment: Appears to be compliment but is opposite.

e.g. In an argument I say: "You're good at debating"
-> good at using words to convince
-> regardless of being right or wrong
-> you are in the wrong

### Sarcasm

Sarcasm: indirect + negative intent

e.g. "I would explain this to you, but I don't have enough crayons."
-> you are not smarter than a child
-> you are stupid
-> you cannot understand

## Lying

### Telling lies

Must be self consistent (to other person's POV).

You use what your opponent knows and fill in the void in a consistent way.

### Without telling lies

1. Sampling bias.
   If a topic is 75% true, 25% false, I can simply sample more from the 25% than then 75%.
2. If the topic is TRUE, I can project it onto something similar but not equivalent.
   After several such transformations, I can use analogies to drive it towards FALSE.
